## Project title
MedGrid Sentinel: fuel-aware hospital microgrids for cities the grid can't protect

## Team
Team MedGrid Sentinel: Jackson Sixtus Abetianbe (solo entry)

## Brief · Track · Setting
Brief #13 "Power for a city the grid can't protect" · Track 2 Digital Solutions & AI · Setting B Non-residential (healthcare)

## Short description (≤ 280 characters)
The generator works; the fuel doesn't come. MedGrid Sentinel is a hospital-microgrid twin and predictive controller that turns every litre the sun saves into hours of critical care. In the real 39-day Maiduguri line outage: 449 → 937 critical-service hours on less diesel.

## Long description (≈ 250 words)
When insurgents cut the Gombe–Damaturu–Maiduguri 330 kV line on 22 September 2024, Maiduguri lost grid power until 31 October. In such a city, hospitals don't run out of generator; they run out of diesel. In December 2022 two Maiduguri hospitals sat dark for days with working generators and empty tanks.

MedGrid Sentinel is a hospital-microgrid digital twin with a fuel-aware predictive controller. Every hour, its fuel-runway governor compares diesel, battery and forecast solar with the energy each care tier needs until the next expected delivery. It defers the least critical loads first and never Tier-1 (ICU, theatres, cold chain). A clinical ride-through reserve keeps the ICU powered through generator failures, and efficient cycling cuts generator hours.

We replayed the real 39-day outage on NASA POWER measured weather for an illustrative 250-bed hospital, with four controllers facing identical fuel deliveries. At 6,000 L/week the status quo keeps critical services on for 449 of 937 hours; Sentinel keeps all 937 and burns 2,841 fewer litres. On identical solar and battery, the software alone adds 179 hours. In a 300-trial Monte Carlo using NREL generator-failure data, the probability that life-critical circuits stay powered through a 14-day outage rises from 0% to 78%.

The economics hold: $704k for 600 kWp + 600 kWh + Sentinel, 3.3-year payback, IRR 30%, −40% diesel (91,654 L/yr). Under energy-as-a-service the hospital pays nothing upfront and saves 20% from month one. It runs offline, with alerts by SMS and USSD on any phone.

## Problem statement
Short: In cities whose transmission lines are repeatedly cut, hospitals lose critical care when their diesel runs out, not because their generators are too small.

Long: A multi-week line outage quadruples a hospital's diesel need (our 250-bed reference: about 3,360 L/week normally) while deliveries, budgets and insecure convoys stay flat. Only 50% of hospitals in sub-Saharan Africa report reliable electricity (WHO et al., 2023). Backup is sized in kW and run on fixed rules; nobody decides, in advance and with numbers, which loads the remaining litres should protect.

## Solution
A 15-minute hospital-microgrid digital twin (16 tiered circuits, PV, LFP storage, generator, diesel tank and resupply) with a predictive controller: the fuel-runway governor, a clinical ride-through reserve, generator cycling, and opportunistic solar. It also includes a Monte Carlo resilience lab, full-year economics, a fleet screen, and an SMS/USSD field channel. It ships as an offline static web app.

## Innovation
Treats diesel logistics, not kW, as the binding constraint, and schedules care tiers against the next convoy. The value of the control software is isolated by running four controllers on identical hardware and fuel. Forecasting is trained and tested on real held-out data and calibrates the controller. Safety-critical dispatch stays deterministic and explainable.

## Technical implementation
TypeScript engine (deterministic, 101 automated tests: power balance for every strategy, diesel mass conservation, NREL reliability reproduction). NASA POWER hourly data for 10 site-years. Next.js static export with a react-three-fiber 3D twin. Every published number is regenerated by `npm run results`.

## Impact
Replay: critical-service hours 449 → 937 (+487), Tier-1 451 → 937, Tier-1 interruptions 12 → 0, empty-tank hours 489 → 0, 2,841 L avoided. Year: 91,654 L diesel and 246 t CO₂ avoided, 259 → 0 life-critical transfer breaks, 1,286 fewer generator hours. 14-day probability of keeping Tier-1 powered: 0% → 78%. One hospital is about 91,250 bed-days a year.

## Sustainability & affordability
3.3-yr payback, IRR 30%, 20-yr NPV +₦553M at 18% after battery/inverter replacement; positive under every sensitivity tested. EaaS: zero hospital CapEx, ₦20.7M/month fee below savings, hospital keeps 20% from month one, provider IRR 23.6%. Energy cost per bed-day falls from ₦8,940 to ₦8,258, so nothing is passed to patients. Maintainable locally; −40% diesel.

## Feasibility
Working prototype on real weather and published reliability data; standards mapped (IEC 60364-7-710, 62446, 62109, 62619, NEMSA). Biggest risk: real loads differ from the model. It's tested by three months of shadow-mode operation on circuit-level metering before any capital (gate: ±15% daily-energy error).

## Scalability
Replication by configuration only. Fleet screen of 10 site-years on measured weather: strong in Maiduguri, Kano, Abuja and Port Harcourt (2.9–4.9 yr payback); viable in Lagos (5.9–6.2 yr). Costs are assumed to fall about 6% per fleet doubling (hypothesis). Transfers to Ghana, Kenya and U.S. hospitals (NFPA 99/110).

## Deployment (6–18 months)
M1–3 metering + shadow mode at one Maiduguri-class hospital; M4–6 engineering design, NEMSA plan, EaaS term sheet with a licensed EPC; M7–12 install and operate with monthly measured-vs-predicted reports; M13–18 sites 2–3.

## Limitations
Simulation, not field-measured performance; illustrative loads; fuel and repair logistics assumed (exposed as sliders); 15-minute resolution (no protection transients); supervisory, not a certified protection system.
