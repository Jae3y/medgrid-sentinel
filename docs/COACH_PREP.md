# Coach Checkpoint Prep

**Ask for:** a coach with hospital power systems / microgrid experience (first choice), or energy-as-a-service finance (second).

## 60-second briefing
Brief #13, Track 2, Setting B. Fuel-aware controller and digital twin for referral hospitals in cities whose transmission is repeatedly cut. Replay of the real 39-day Maiduguri outage on measured weather: critical-service hours go from 449 to 937 on less diesel; the software alone adds 179 h on identical hardware. Monte Carlo with NREL generator data: P(Tier-1 kept for 14 days) goes from 0% to 78%. CapEx $704k, 3.3-yr payback, EaaS with zero hospital CapEx. Solo entry.

## Questions to ask
1. Is our Tier-1/2/3 mapping (ICU, theatres, cold chain, oxygen, ED as Tier 1) how a Nigerian hospital works department would classify circuits? What are we missing (sterilisation? dialysis?)
2. Is 6,000 L/week (1.8× normal) a credible constrained-supply assumption for a NE Nigeria hospital during a line outage? What do fuel contracts actually look like there?
3. Would a hospital accept automatic deferral of Tier-3 loads (cooling, laundry), or must every shed be operator-confirmed? We plan shadow mode first.
4. EaaS: is an 80/20 savings split realistic for Nigerian public hospitals? Which ESCOs operate in the North-East?
5. Which single number should lead the pitch: 449 → 937 h, 0% → 78%, or 3.3-yr payback?
6. Are 15-minute steps defensible for judges, given that we count 15-second transfer breaks separately?
7. Anything in the deck or one-pager that reads as overclaiming?
